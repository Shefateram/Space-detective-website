CASE 001

The Little Helicopter That Could

April 19, 2021 – January 18, 2024
Location: Jezero Crater, Mars

Chapter 1: The Unexpected Passenger

Meet Ingenuity, a tiny robotic helicopter weighing just 1.8 kilograms.

It travelled all the way from Earth to Mars tucked beneath the Perseverance rover. Its mission? To prove that powered flight was possible on another planet.

Chapter 2: A Flight Beyond Imagination

On April 19, 2021, Ingenuity made history. It became the first aircraft to achieve powered, controlled flight on another planet.

During its first flight, it climbed approximately 3 metres above the Martian surface and remained airborne for 39.1 seconds.

Chapter 3: An Unexpected Career

Ingenuity was originally designed for just five experimental flights over 30 days. But it had other plans!

It eventually completed 72 flights, travelled approximately 17 kilometres and reached a maximum altitude of 24 metres.

It also became an aerial scout, helping the Perseverance team observe Martian terrain from above.

Chapter 4: The Final Flight

On January 18, 2024, Ingenuity completed its 72nd and final flight.

During the flight, its navigation system encountered difficulties, and the helicopter sustained damage to its rotor blades. It could no longer fly.

Chapter 5: A Legacy Beyond Mars

Ingenuity's mission may have ended, but its achievements changed the future of planetary exploration.

It proved that powered flight was possible in the thin atmosphere of Mars and showed how aerial vehicles could help explore other worlds.

Sometimes, even the smallest explorers leave the biggest legacies.

CASE 002

Listening to Mars's Heartbeat

NASA's InSight Mission
Location: Elysium Planitia, Mars

🔴 Meet InSight — the robot scientist designed to investigate the mysteries beneath Mars.

Chapter 1: A Different Kind of Explorer

Most robots on Mars have wheels so they can drive around and look at rocks. Not you!

Your name is InSight, and you are a stationary robot scientist with three sturdy legs.

Instead of looking at the outside of Mars, your job is to check Mars's "vital signs"— its pulse, temperature, and reflexes— to figure out what is deep inside the planet.

Chapter 2: Putting the Stethoscope Down

You land on a wide, flat plain called Elysium Planitia.

First, you open your two big round solar panels like giant fans to soak up the sunshine.

Next, you use your 5-foot-long robotic arm to carefully pick up a copper-colored dome from your deck and set it straight onto the red ground.

Inside that dome is an ultra-sensitive seismometer— a tool so good at listening that it can feel vibrations smaller than the width of a single atom!

Chapter 3: Rumble and Whoosh!

You stay completely still and listen. For days, all you hear is the spooky, low whoosh of Martian wind blowing past your solar panels at 15 miles per hour.

Then, suddenly—rumble, shake! You feel a "marsquake" rippling through the ground!

Over four years, you detect over 1,300 marsquakes, including some caused by space rocks crashing into Mars.

Thanks to your careful listening, scientists learn that Mars has a big liquid core and a lumpy rocky mantle underneath its red crust.

CASE 003

The Twin Detectives of Mars

NASA's Spirit and Opportunity Missions
January 2004 – 2018
Locations: Gusev Crater and Meridiani Planum, Mars

🔴 A view from the Spirit mission's landing site on the Martian surface.

Chapter 1: Identical Twins on Opposite Sides of a Planet

Meet Spirit and Opportunity, two identical twin robot geologists built by NASA.

Even though they look exactly alike—with six wheels, flat solar-panel "wings," and tall camera "heads"— they don't get to explore together.

In January 2004, NASA lands them on completely opposite sides of Mars!

Spirit bounces down in giant airbags inside Gusev Crater, which looks like an ancient lakebed.

Three weeks later, Opportunity rolls right into a small crater on a flat plain called Meridiani Planum.

Chapter 2: Hunting for Water Clues

Your mission as one of the twins is to act like a rock detective and search for clues that liquid water once flowed on Mars.

You use your robotic arm to brush dust off strange rocks and peek at them with a magnifying camera.

Almost right away, Opportunity spots tiny, round gray pebbles nicknamed "blueberries" scattered in the dirt—a huge clue that water once soaked the ground!

Meanwhile, Spirit climbs rocky hills and finds minerals that only form in hot water and steam.

Together, the twins prove that dry, dusty Mars used to be a wet world long ago!

Chapter 3: The 90-Day Mission That Lasted 14 Years!

Engineers originally thought the twins would only survive for about three months before red dust covered their solar panels and drained their batteries.

Instead, helpful Martian winds sweep by like giant invisible brooms, blowing dust off their panels so they can keep recharging!

Spirit explores for over six years and drives nearly five miles before getting stuck in soft sand.

Opportunity keeps rolling like a marathon runner, traveling over 28 miles and sending back hundreds of thousands of photos for more than 14 years—

Until a massive, planet-wide dust storm finally darkens the sky in 2018 and puts the brave rover to sleep.
